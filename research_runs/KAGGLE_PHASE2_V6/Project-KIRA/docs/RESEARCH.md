### Why Sparkov

It does **four** jobs at once, which is why one dataset is enough:

1. **Calibration source** — fit the simulator's archetype parameters to its real marginals
2. **External reality anchor** — evaluate our detector on it (fixes drawback F-07)
3. **C2ST reference** — the "real" side of the realism discriminator
4. **TSTR test set** — train on synthetic, test on real

It carries `cc_num`, `merchant`, `category`, `amt`, `trans_date_trans_time`,
`lat`/`long`, `city_pop`, `job`, `dob`, `is_fraud`. Entity ids plus timestamps is
exactly what behavioural fidelity measurement requires. Generated by the Sparkov
Data Generation tool (Brandon Harris).

## 2. Behavioural fidelity — the most valuable finding

**Source:** arXiv 2604.13125, *"Synthetic Tabular Generators Fail to Preserve
Behavioral Fraud Patterns: A Benchmark on Temporal, Velocity, and Multi-Account
Signals"*.

**What it does:** benchmarks CTGAN, TVAE, GaussianCopula and TabularARGN on
IEEE-CIS and Amazon Fraud across four behavioural axes, scored with a degradation
ratio calibrated to real-data variability where **1.0 = matches real data**.

| Axis | What it measures |
|---|---|
| P1 | inter-event timing |
| P2 | burst structure |
| P3 | multi-account graph motifs |
| P4 | velocity-rule trigger rates |

**Results — the baselines we have to beat:**

| Generator | Dataset | Degradation ratio |
|---|---|---|
| TVAE | IEEE-CIS | 24.4× |
| CTGAN | IEEE-CIS | ~30× |
| GaussianCopula | IEEE-CIS | 39.0× |
| Row-independent generators | Amazon Fraud | 81.6–99.7× |
| TabularARGN | Amazon Fraud | 17.2× |

**Why this matters so much to us:** it is independent published evidence that the
"just run CTGAN or a diffusion model" approach — which is what most competing teams
will do — **structurally fails** on exactly the signals fraud detection depends on.
The paper argues row-independent generators cannot reproduce multi-account motifs
or the positive autocorrelation fraud detection needs.

Our stateful simulator should beat those numbers **by construction**. That converts
a hand-wave ("our data is realistic") into a defensible headline: *our generator
scores N× on the same four axes where published generators score 17–99×.*

**The normalisation trick — do not skip it.** Split the real data in half and
compute each metric between the two halves. That is the natural variability floor.
Then report `metric(real, synth) / metric(real_a, real_b)`. Without this every
fidelity number is uninterpretable because you cannot tell generator error from
sampling noise.

## 3. Detectability — C2ST

**Method:** label real rows 0 and synthetic rows 1, train a discriminator
(LightGBM), report cross-validated AUC. Standard practice; implemented in the
SDMetrics library.

- AUC ≈ 0.50 → synthetic is indistinguishable from real. The honest pass.
- AUC ≈ 1.00 → trivially fake.

**The part that makes it a tool rather than a score:** read the discriminator's
SHAP values. The top features are precisely the artefacts giving you away — a
rounded amount distribution, an impossible timestamp granularity, a categorical
that never takes its rare values. Fix, regenerate, re-run, watch the AUC fall.

Run at **two levels**. Row-level is weak and easy to pass. Entity-level (aggregate
per-account into a behavioural profile, then discriminate) is much harder and far
more meaningful for fraud.

Related: **TSTR** (train on synthetic, test on real) measures utility rather than
similarity, and is the strongest answer to "so what?".

## 4. Class imbalance — why we do not use SMOTE

**Sources:** an IJCT 2024 study of posterior-probability bias under SMOTE
oversampling (HistGradientBoosting and Logistic Regression on the Kaggle credit-card
fraud dataset), extending earlier work by Dal Pozzolo et al.

**Finding:** applying SMOTE as the sole imbalance intervention drove Logistic
Regression false alarms from **35 to 5,775** across five cross-validation folds
while ROC-AUC stayed essentially unchanged at **0.9806**. Resampling distorts
posterior class probabilities while preserving ranking order, which makes the
default threshold suboptimal and inflates false positives.

**Why this decides it for us:** our whole decision policy is cost-sensitive and
therefore depends on **calibrated** probabilities. A method that wrecks calibration
while flattering AUC is exactly wrong for this architecture.

**Decision:** `scale_pos_weight` plus threshold optimisation. SMOTE appears only as
an ablation, reported on **ECE and FPR** — not AUC, which is the metric that hides
the damage. Recorded as D-004.

**Honest caveat:** the broader literature is mixed. Some studies find no
significant AUC difference between baseline, SMOTE and weighting for tree models.
Our argument is specifically about calibration, not about AUC, and the report
should say so rather than overclaiming.

## 5. Constrained adversarial attacks on tabular models

**Sources:** arXiv 2406.00775 (*Constrained Adaptive Attack*, NeurIPS 2024),
arXiv 2311.04503, and TabularBench (arXiv 2408.07579, `serval-uni-lu/tabularbench`).

**What exists:**

- **MOEVA** — a genetic multi-objective attack optimising classifier error,
  perturbation size, and constraint violations. Works on any model. Known weakness:
  computationally expensive.
- **CAPGD / CAA** — gradient-based, up to 5× faster than MOEVA, outperforming
  alternatives in 17 of 20 settings.
- A **constraint parser** where relationships between features are written in a
  human-readable format.

**What this means for us — the load-bearing conclusion:**

> **CAA and CAPGD are gradient-based and will not work against LightGBM.** For a
> gradient-boosted tree the genetic path is the only option, and its documented
> weakness is exactly the thing that threatens our timeline: compute cost.

So the mitigation is engineering, not algorithm choice: **score the entire
population in one batched `predict()` call**. That is the first thing to build in
BLOCK 4. `pymoo` NSGA-II (v0.6.2, actively maintained, free) is a stretch goal, not
the baseline.

The constraint-parser idea is worth borrowing: our mutability mask should be a
declarative spec, not scattered `if` statements.

### Prior art the novelty claim must respect

- Evasion attacks against banking fraud detection systems are documented from 2020
  (Carminati et al., USENIX RAID).
- Multi-objective evolutionary adversarial attacks on tabular data are an
  established line of work.
- LLM-driven red/blue closed-loop co-evolution frameworks were published in
  2025–26, including results shaped exactly like the one we intend to produce
  (attack success 72% → 5% over six iterations).

**"Adaptive red team hardens blue team" is not the novel part.** Narrow the claim
to what is actually ours: the payment-specific stateful world, the mutability mask,
the agentic mandate layer, and the measurement protocol.

### Withdrawn — do not cite

**arXiv 2509.22850, "Boundary on the Table: Efficient Black-Box Decision-Based
Attacks for Structured Data."** It fits our threat model almost perfectly
(label-only black-box, small query budgets, >90% success). It was **withdrawn by
its author** on 23 November 2025. Citing a withdrawn paper is worse than having no
citation.

## 6. Mastercard context — the agentic angle

Both verified against Mastercard's own newsroom and investor relations.

| Claim | Detail | Date |
|---|---|---|
| **Verifiable Intent** | Open-source, standards-based framework for agentic commerce. Links a consumer's identity, their specific instructions, and the transaction outcome into a single tamper-resistant record — a cryptographic audit trail all parties can consult in a dispute. Uses **selective disclosure** to share only the minimum needed with each party. | March 2026 |
| **Agent Pay for Machines** | Infrastructure for high-frequency, low-latency, low-value payments executed by agents and machines, with credentialing, permissioning and guaranteed settlement across cards, stablecoins and other rails. | June 2026 |
| **AP2** | Mastercard joined the Agent Payments Protocol as a launch partner; an AP2-compliant agent can pay over Mastercard rails with the AP2 mandate envelope wrapping a Mastercard Agentic Token. | Sept 2025 |

**Why this is the strongest strategic call in the project:** determining whether an
AI agent's eventual action remains consistent with what the user authorised is a
real, current, unsolved problem that Mastercard is actively building
infrastructure for. Our intent-drift engine is a prototype mechanism inspired by
that problem.

**What we must never claim:** that our `intent_drift_score` is Mastercard's
production scoring mechanism, that we integrate with Verifiable Intent, or that we
implement AP2.

Wider context worth one line in the report: an IMF note (2026) frames agentic
payments as three layers — intent formation, authorisation and control, settlement —
and observes that traditional fraud models rely on *human* behavioural patterns,
which become ineffective when transactions are initiated by autonomous agents.
That is the gap our R8 family targets.

## 7. Free compute — what the limits actually are

| Resource | Limit | Verified |
|---|---|---|
| Kaggle GPU | ~30 h/week, 12 h/session, T4 or P100 | yes |
| **Kaggle CPU** | 4 cores, ~30 GB RAM, 12 h/session, **separate allocation — does not consume the GPU quota** | yes |
| HF Spaces free (`cpu-basic`) | 2 vCPU, **16 GB RAM**, 50 GB ephemeral disk, Docker SDK, sleeps after 48 h idle and auto-wakes on visit (~30 s) | yes |
| HF persistent storage | paid only. Free Spaces have ephemeral disk — data is lost on restart | yes |
| HF Dataset repos | free, git-LFS backed. Usable as an artifact store | yes |
| HF ZeroGPU | free tier exhausts in ~5 min/day. **We do not use it.** | yes |

**The conclusion that matters:** this project's P0 path needs **zero GPU hours**.
Simulation, LightGBM, genetic search, SHAP and all statistics are CPU-bound. Kaggle
CPU sessions do not touch the GPU quota, so the team's GPU hours stay intact for
other projects.

For comparison, rejected hosting options: Render free is 512 MB RAM and sleeps
after 15 minutes with a 30–50 s cold start (a demo killer); Railway's free tier is
a one-time expiring credit; Fly.io has no free tier in 2026. HF Spaces at 16 GB and
a 48 h sleep window is decisively the right choice.
